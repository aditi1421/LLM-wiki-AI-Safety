# Will AGI Benefit Ordinary People?

The central question: once AI systems can perform most economically valuable work, will the resulting wealth be broadly shared or concentrated among a few powerful actors?

## Positions

### Default Utopia
**Claim:** AGI will free humans from drudgery. Without the need for jobs, people will self-actualize, pursue art, and live in abundance.

**Proponents:** Common in AI labs and parts of the AI safety community. Drago recounts a conversation with a well-connected AI figure who said: "We won't need jobs — we'll be free to self-actualize. We'll pursue meaningful goals and write poetry." ([[drago-2025-intelligence-curse]])

**Weakness:** Assumes wealth distribution happens automatically. Drago: "You do not get to utopian poetry writing by having faith that someone else will figure it out."

### New Jobs Will Emerge
**Claim:** Every technological revolution creates new categories of jobs. AI will be no different — roles we can't yet imagine will replace those that are automated.

**Proponents:** Common among economists and policymakers. One AI policy senior figure: "We'll have new jobs — maybe everyone will work in AI policy!"

**Weakness:** Previous technologies were tools that augmented human workers. If AGI is domain-agnostic and fully substitutive, the historical pattern may not repeat. Also doesn't address the [[intelligence-curse]] incentive problem — even if some jobs exist, there's no incentive to train people for them.

### Intelligence Curse (Neo-Feudalism)
**Claim:** By default, AGI will create [[rentier-state]]-style incentives at global scale. Powerful actors will stop investing in people because people no longer generate returns. The outcome is a stable equilibrium of concentrated wealth and mass poverty.

**Proponents:** Luke Drago, Rudolf Laine ([[drago-2025-intelligence-curse]])

**Weakness:** The analogy to rentier states may not fully transfer — democratic states can tax AI companies, and political accountability mechanisms may hold. Solutions are not yet articulated in detail.

### Gradual Disempowerment
**Claim:** Even without dramatic AGI takeover, incremental AI capability increases erode human agency across economy, culture, and governance. Institutions that currently stay aligned with human welfare through human participation lose that alignment as AI displaces humans from key roles. The result is existential catastrophe through accumulated small losses, not a single event.

**Proponents:** Jan Kulveit, Raymond Douglas, Nora Ammann, Deger Turan, David Krueger, David Duvenaud ([[kulveit-2025-gradual-disempowerment]])

**Weakness:** Primarily a conceptual framing — quantitative modeling of the amplification dynamics is limited. Institutions also have dampening and self-correcting mechanisms that may slow or reverse the process.

**Relationship to Intelligence Curse:** Complementary. Drago focuses on incentives (why powerful actors stop investing in people), Kulveit et al. focus on mechanisms (how institutional alignment channels degrade). Both conclude [[intent-alignment]] is insufficient.

## Open Questions

- Can democratic governance counteract the curse before it locks in?
- Is the "AGI as resource" framing accurate, or will AGI remain tool-like for longer?
- What role does the transition period (pre-full-automation) play in determining outcomes?
- Does comparative advantage still apply in a world with AGI?
