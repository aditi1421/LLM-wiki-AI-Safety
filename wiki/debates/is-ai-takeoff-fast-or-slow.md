# Is AI Takeoff Fast or Slow?

How quickly will AI capabilities improve from "useful but limited" to "surpasses humans at everything"? This debate has major implications for AI safety — fast takeoff means less time to detect problems, implement governance, and adapt society.

## Positions

### Fast Takeoff (months to ~2 years)
**Claim:** The transition from partial to full automation will be rapid, potentially faster than institutions can respond.

**Key arguments:**
- The difficulty gap between 20%-AI and 100%-AI may be small (chimps→humans = ~3X brain size, ~10X compute)
- AI automating AI R&D creates accelerating feedback loops ([[intelligence-explosion]])
- Investment surges once economic value of AI becomes clear (~$10tr/year at 20%-AI)
- Effective compute could rise 5-100X per year during takeoff

**Proponents:** Eliezer Yudkowsky (qualitative arguments), portions of Davidson's model ([[davidson-2023-compute-centric-takeoff]])

### Slow Takeoff (5+ years to decades)
**Claim:** AI will automate tasks gradually, with significant gaps between partial and full automation.

**Key arguments:**
- AI has strong comparative advantages at certain tasks but not others — early automation doesn't generalize
- Hardware progress approaches physical limits
- Talent bottlenecks and parallelization limits slow R&D scaling ("nine mothers can't make a baby in one month")
- Political resistance and deployment lags slow real-world impact

**Proponents:** Paul Christiano (2018 blog post), portions of Davidson's analysis

### Continuous But Fast (Davidson's key insight)
**Claim:** Takeoff can be **continuous** (no sudden jumps) and still very fast. These are not contradictory.

Even without discontinuities, Davidson puts ~15% on <1 year takeoff and ~60% on <5 year takeoff in the continuous scenario. The slope of improvement can be steep enough for rapid transition even if progress never "jumps." ([[davidson-2023-compute-centric-takeoff]])

This challenges the common framing where "continuous" = "gradual" = "lots of warning."

## Key Uncertainties

- **The effective FLOP gap:** How much harder is 100%-AI than 20%-AI? This is the single biggest determinant.
- **AI R&D automation:** How easily can AI accelerate its own development?
- **Investment dynamics:** How fast will spending scale up when AI's value becomes clear?
- **Comparative advantage:** Does AI have narrow strengths that make early tasks much easier than later tasks?

## Implications

| Takeoff Speed | Time to Respond | Safety Difficulty |
|--------------|----------------|------------------|
| <1 year | Almost none | Extremely hard — must solve alignment before takeoff |
| 1-5 years | Limited | Hard — narrow window for course correction |
| 5-10 years | Moderate | Challenging but feasible if starting now |
| >10 years | Substantial | Manageable with sustained effort |

The [[intelligence-curse]] is harder to avoid with fast takeoff — economic and governance structures can't adapt quickly enough.
