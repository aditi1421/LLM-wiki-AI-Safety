# The Intelligence Curse

**Author:** Luke Drago | **Published:** January 4, 2025 | **Platform:** LessWrong (linkpost from Substack)

## Summary

Drago argues that AGI will function more like a natural resource (oil) than a tool (laptops), creating what he calls the [[intelligence-curse]] — a phenomenon where powerful actors (states, corporations) lose their economic incentive to invest in people once AI can replace human labor more cheaply and reliably.

## Key Claims

1. **AGI resembles a resource, not a tool.** Unlike laptops which augment workers, AGI can fully substitute them — cheaper, faster, more predictable. This makes it analogous to oil: concentrated, extractable, and independent of human capital. ([[economic-displacement-by-ai]])

2. **The [[resource-curse]] analogy holds.** [[rentier-state|Rentier states]] that derive wealth from resources rather than citizens tend to underinvest in human welfare. AGI-powered economies will face the same incentive structure but worse — you can't "run out" of AI like you can oil, removing the pressure to diversify.

3. **Powerful actors care about people for instrumental reasons.** States invest in education, infrastructure, and rights because productive citizens generate tax revenue and political stability. Remove that dependency, and the incentive to invest vanishes.

4. **The default outcome is neo-feudalism.** Without intervention, a small number of post-AGI elites control all powerful actors while everyone else struggles to meet basic needs — a stable equilibrium like the worst rentier states.

5. **[[intent-alignment]] alone is insufficient.** The AI safety community focuses on making AGI controllable, but controllable AGI in the hands of actors subject to the intelligence curse doesn't produce good outcomes. "You can't just plan for AGI — you have to plan the day after."

## Evidence and Sources Cited

- **Rentier state theory:** Beblawi & Luciani (1987), Karl (1997), Acemoglu, Robinson & Verdier (2004), Mesquita & Smith (2009)
- **Welfare provision in oil states:** Eibl & Hertog — comparing Oman (expansive welfare) vs. Equatorial Guinea (kleptocratic extraction) despite similar per-capita rents
- **DRC paradox:** $24 trillion in minerals, 73.5% living on <$2.15/day (World Bank 2024)
- **Rudolf Laine's "Capital, AGI, and human ambition"** — companion piece arguing labor-replacing AI shifts power away from humans

## Proposed Solutions (future post)

1. **Governance:** Democratic accountability — voting to counteract the curse
2. **Innovation:** Tech that increases human agency and economic relevance

## Limitations

- Solutions are deferred to a future post — this piece is diagnosis, not prescription
- The resource curse analogy may not fully transfer — states can tax AI companies in ways they can't tax foreign oil buyers
- Doesn't engage deeply with comparative advantage arguments (acknowledged, deferred)
