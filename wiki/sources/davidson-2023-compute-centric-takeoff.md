# What a Compute-Centric Framework Says About AI Takeoff Speeds

**Author:** Tom Davidson (Open Philanthropy) | **Published:** January 23, 2023 | **Platform:** AI Alignment Forum

## Summary

Davidson presents a quantitative model for AI takeoff speeds — how quickly AI capabilities improve as we approach and surpass human-level AI. The model uses a [[compute-centric-framework]] inspired by the Bio Anchors report, where AI capabilities increase continuously with more training compute and better algorithms. The report accompanies an interactive model at takeoffspeeds.com.

## Key Contributions

1. **The "effective FLOP gap" metric.** Defines [[ai-takeoff-speeds]] in terms of time from "20%-AI" (AI that can readily automate 20% of cognitive tasks) to "100%-AI" (all cognitive tasks). Best guess: 10,000X more effective compute needed for 100%-AI vs 20%-AI.

2. **Takeoff probability estimates** (conditional on AGI by 2100):
   - ~10% to <3 month takeoff
   - ~25% to <1 year takeoff
   - ~50% to <3 year takeoff
   - ~80% to <10 year takeoff

3. **Two major model innovations** over Bio Anchors:
   - Algorithmic and hardware progress modeled via semi-endogenous growth models (spending accelerates near AGI, driving faster progress)
   - Pre-AGI systems automating economic tasks and AI R&D tasks, creating feedback loops

4. **AGI to [[superintelligence]] in <1 year.** Once AGI exists, massive AI labour available for AI R&D drives rapid capability gains.

5. **Capabilities vs impact takeoff distinction.** If aligned, impact takeoff probably slower (~10 years) due to deployment lags, political economy, caution. If not aligned, impact takeoff could be sudden — misaligned AIs forcibly circumventing human deployment decisions.

## Arguments For Fast Takeoff (<3 years)

- May not be much harder to develop 100%-AI than 20%-AI (chimps→humans = only 3X brain size)
- Algorithmic progress already fast (10-16 month doubling)
- Spending rises dramatically once 20%-AI demonstrates ~$10tr/year GDP value
- AI automating AI R&D creates acceleration feedback loop (1.3-1.8X speedup)
- Effective compute could rise 5-100X per year during takeoff

## Arguments For Slow Takeoff (>5 years)

- AI may have strong comparative advantages at certain tasks but not others — 20% could be automated long before 100%
- Hardware progress may slow near physical limits
- Hard to convert more $ into faster progress (talent bottlenecks, parallelization limits)
- Deployment lags and political resistance

## Key Finding: Continuous ≠ Slow

Even without discontinuities, takeoff could be fast. The slope of continuous improvement could be steep enough for <1 year takeoff. This challenges the common assumption that "continuous progress" implies "slow takeoff."

## Relation to Other Work

- Provides quantitative framework for Yudkowsky's [[intelligence-explosion]] Microeconomics
- Extends Paul Christiano's argument that takeoff is likely continuous, but shows it can still be fast
- ~6% probability assigned to substantial discontinuity around human-level range

## Limitations

- Author's probabilities described as "not robust" — could change significantly with new evidence
- Model excludes certain discontinuities and deployment frictions
- Median AGI training requirements (1e36 FLOP) are high; lower requirements make takeoff significantly faster
