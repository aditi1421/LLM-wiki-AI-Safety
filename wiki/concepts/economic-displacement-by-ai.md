# Economic Displacement by AI

The replacement of human labor by AI systems across economically valuable tasks. Unlike previous technological revolutions that augmented human productivity, AGI-level systems may fully substitute human workers — doing tasks cheaper, faster, and more reliably.

## The Tool vs. Resource Distinction

Drago ([[drago-2025-intelligence-curse]]) draws a critical distinction:

**Past technology = tools:** Laptops, the internet, and software augmented human workers. You needed analysts to use the laptops. The technology increased per-worker productivity, which increased demand for workers.

**AGI = resource:** AGI can perform tasks independently — no human operator required. Like oil, it slots in reliably, performs predictably, and can be scaled without hiring. This makes it substitutive rather than augmentative.

## Why This Time May Be Different

- AGI is **domain-agnostic** — not superhuman in one field but competent across all fields
- AI removes the **talent bottleneck** — no need to find, judge, or retain scarce human talent
- Competitive pressure forces adoption — companies that don't replace humans lose to those that do
- Aschenbrenner: "That doesn't require believing in sci-fi; it just requires believing in straight lines on a graph"

## Current Evidence

Pre-AGI systems are already shrinking analyst classes, changing hiring strategies, and triggering layoffs. These systems will only improve over time.

## The Deeper Problem

The standard economic response — "new jobs will emerge" — may not apply. The [[intelligence-curse]] argument goes further than job loss: even if some human roles persist, the economic incentive to *fund* human development (education, infrastructure, welfare) disappears when AI generates the revenue instead.
