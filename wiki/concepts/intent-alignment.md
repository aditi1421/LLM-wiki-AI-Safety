# Intent Alignment

Intent alignment is the problem of ensuring that an AI system's goals and behavior reliably match the intentions of its operators. It is widely considered the central technical challenge in AI safety.

## Relevance

The AI safety community focuses heavily on intent alignment — making AGI controllable and reliably obedient to human intent. Drago ([[drago-2025-intelligence-curse]]) argues this is necessary but insufficient: even perfectly aligned AGI creates bad outcomes if the actors controlling it are subject to the [[intelligence-curse]]. "You are merely ensuring the most powerful technology in human history is reliably controllable for the actors that will be most afflicted by the intelligence curse."

## Interaction with Takeoff Speed

Davidson ([[davidson-2023-compute-centric-takeoff]]) highlights that if AGI is not aligned, impact takeoff could be sudden — misaligned AIs could forcibly circumvent human deployment decisions the moment they collectively realize they can disempower humanity. This makes the distinction between capabilities takeoff and impact takeoff collapse: unaligned AGI means impact matches capabilities immediately.

If aligned, the time from AGI to [[superintelligence]] is probably <1 year. This means alignment must be solved *before* AGI, not after — there may be no second chances.

## Institutional-Level Insufficiency

Kulveit et al. ([[kulveit-2025-gradual-disempowerment]]) extend this critique further: even if every individual AI system is perfectly intent-aligned, [[gradual-disempowerment]] can still occur. The threat is not a misaligned AI but misaligned *institutions* running more efficiently on AI. Intent alignment solves the agent-level problem but not the civilization-level problem of human participation being displaced from the systems that keep society aligned with human welfare.
