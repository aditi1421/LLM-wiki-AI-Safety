# Intelligence Curse

The intelligence curse is the phenomenon where powerful actors (states, corporations, bureaucracies) that adopt labor-replacing AGI lose their economic incentive to invest in people. Coined by Luke Drago in January 2025. ([[drago-2025-intelligence-curse]])

## Core Mechanism

Today, states and corporations invest in people because people generate returns — through taxes, labor, innovation, and consumption. This creates a feedback loop: invest in education and infrastructure → citizens become more productive → state earns more tax revenue → reinvest.

AGI breaks this loop. If AI systems can do any economically valuable work cheaper, faster, and more reliably than humans, powerful actors no longer receive returns on human investment. The feedback loop collapses.

## Analogy to the Resource Curse

The concept draws directly from [[resource-curse]] theory. [[rentier-state|Rentier states]] that earn revenue from natural resources rather than citizen productivity tend to underinvest in human welfare. The intelligence curse applies the same logic at larger scale:

| Resource Curse | Intelligence Curse |
|---------------|-------------------|
| Revenue from oil/minerals | Revenue from AI systems |
| No need to develop taxable workforce | No need to develop productive workers |
| Wealth concentrated in resource controllers | Wealth concentrated in AI labs and adopters |
| Some pressure to diversify (oil runs out) | **No diversification pressure** (AI improves over time) |

The key difference: the intelligence curse is worse because AGI doesn't deplete, doesn't require transition, and improves with each generation.

## Default Outcomes

Without intervention, Drago predicts:
- Companies fire workers and never rehire — AI is cheaper and better
- States shift revenue base from income tax to corporate tax on AI companies
- Public investment in education, infrastructure, and welfare loses its ROI justification
- A stable equilibrium emerges: a few elites control AI-powered actors, mass poverty for the rest

## Relationship to AI Safety

The intelligence curse challenges the assumption that solving [[intent-alignment]] leads to good outcomes. Even perfectly aligned AGI, if controlled by actors subject to the curse, produces a world that doesn't invest in people. Safety work ensures AGI is controllable — but controllable for whom, and toward what ends?

## Open Questions

- Can democratic governance counteract the curse before it locks in?
- Does the analogy hold if AI companies can be taxed differently than resource extraction?
- What technologies could keep humans economically relevant post-AGI?
